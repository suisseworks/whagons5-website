Horizontal directional drilling

Trenchless installation of conduit, pipe and cable under roads, driveways, landscape and waterways.

Request a bidCall (951) 603-0745

When to bore instead of trench

Directional boring installs conduit, pipe or cable along a steered path from a drill rig at the surface, without an open trench. It is the usual method under roads and driveways, through finished landscape and under waterways, and it works in a wide range of soil conditions.

Part of a complete job

Most of our bores are one part of a larger scope that we also self-perform:

  • Before the bore: utility locating and hydro-vac potholing.
  • After it: conduit rodding, verification and documentation.
  • At the pits: concrete and asphalt restoration.

What we install

  • Telecommunications conduit and fiber
  • Power cable conduit
  • Water lines
  • Sewer lines
  • Gas lines
  • Environmental remediation casings

How a bore job runs

  1. Route planning, estimating and permits, handled in-house by the team that will build it.

  2. We locate the existing utilities and expose them by hydro-vac potholing to confirm their depth before drilling.

  3. The rig steers a pilot bore along the planned path, then reams it to size for the product going in.

  4. Conduit, pipe or inner-duct is pulled back through the finished bore in one continuous run.

  5. We rod, verify and document the conduit, and our own crews restore the concrete and asphalt at the pits.

Common questions

When is directional drilling a better choice than trenching?

Boring makes sense for road and driveway crossings, finished landscape, waterways and anywhere restoration costs need to stay low. For large-diameter pipe or many conduits in one run, open trench is often the better choice. We do both and will recommend the method that fits the job.

Do you work as the prime contractor or as a subcontractor?

Both. We contract directly with owners and utilities, and we work as a subcontractor to general contractors and engineering firms.

Where do you work?

Our office and yard are in Norco, in the Inland Empire. We are licensed in California, Arizona and Nevada.

Can you handle permitting and planning?

Yes. Route planning, estimating, permit acquisition, project management and scheduling are all done in-house.
