Pipeline & Riser Engineering

Pipelines and risers designed to code and built to last: onshore transmission lines, offshore export pipelines from the shelf to ultra-deepwater, flowlines and fixed risers.

Talk to an engineer See capabilities

One team, every phase of the design.

Our engineers hold degrees in civil, mechanical, structural, geotechnical and ocean engineering. Working as one team, they take a pipeline from conceptual design through front-end engineering design (FEED) and detailed design, then support it through fabrication, testing and installation.

We work with you to set the basis of design and the technical specifications, coordinate geophysical, geotechnical and metocean surveys, and work with your operations team on a field development plan that accounts for existing and future assets.

Systems we design

  • Onshore transmission pipelines
  • Offshore export pipelines, from shallow water to ultra-deepwater
  • Offshore flowlines: single pipe, pipe-in-pipe and flexible
  • Fixed risers and tie-in spools

Challenges we take on

  • High-pressure, high-temperature (HP/HT) systems
  • Active seismic regions and extreme terrain
  • On-bottom stability of large-diameter lines in shallow water
  • Pipeline repair systems, in deep and shallow water

Through every phase of the project.

  1. Concept

    • Route and corridor selection
    • Preliminary wall thickness and material selection
    • Field development planning with your operations team

  2. FEED

    • Basis of design and technical specifications
    • Survey and metocean data coordination
    • Stability, span and expansion screening

  3. Detailed design

    • Wall thickness, stability, cathodic protection and crossings
    • Global buckling, walking and fatigue
    • Alignment sheets, layouts and riser drawings

  4. Fabrication & installation

    • Engineering support through fabrication and testing
    • Installation analysis and procedure review
    • Offshore and field support

  5. Operation

    • Re-qualification and life extension, through our Integrity Management team

Technical capabilities.

Open any item for a short description of the work.

Wall thickness design

Wall thickness for pressure containment, collapse and propagating buckling, checked against the governing code for every route segment and for installation, hydrotest and operation.

On-bottom stability analysis

Lateral and vertical stability under wave and current loading, using the AGA / PRCI on-bottom stability methods, to set weight coating and stabilization needs.

Pipeline span analysis

Allowable span lengths against static and vortex-induced vibration (VIV) fatigue limits, screening of the spans along the surveyed route, and mitigation design where it is needed.

Global buckling and walking analysis

Lateral buckling and pipeline walking assessments for high-temperature, high-pressure lines, progressing from screening to refined models on the real 3D seabed.

Pressure and thermal expansion analysis

Expansion at each pipeline end, and the loads it puts on PLETs, tie-in spools and risers.

Pipeline and riser fatigue analysis

Fatigue damage from installation, operating cycles, VIV and flow transients, combined into one cumulative total at every critical location.

Buckle arrestor design

Arrestor sizing and spacing to stop a propagating buckle, with local finite-element analysis where the standard formulas run out.

Fixed riser and tie-in spool design

Riser and spool layout, flexibility and stress analysis, riser supports (guides and clamps) and riser hang-off forgings.

Pipeline crossing design

Crossings of existing pipelines and cables, with the separation and supports each one needs.

Material and coating selection

Line pipe grade, coating and insulation chosen for the product, the temperature and the installation method.

PLET, PLEM and in-line structure design

Pipeline end terminations, pipeline end manifolds and in-line structures, designed for installation and operating loads.

Pipeline and umbilical route selection

Routes that balance seabed hazards, existing infrastructure, constructability and cost.

Pipe-soil interaction analysis

Axial, lateral and vertical soil resistance at low, best and high estimates, feeding the buckling, walking and span analyses.

Cathodic protection design

Anode sizing and spacing for the design life of the pipeline.

Bottom roughness analysis

The pipeline's response as laid over the surveyed seabed profile, to find spans and high-stress locations before they find you.
