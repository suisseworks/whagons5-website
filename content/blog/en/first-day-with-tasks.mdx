In Whagons, every request, repair, room and round is a task: it has a place, someone who owns it and a status everyone can see. On your first day you only need four things. Where your tasks are, how to report something, how to move a task from new to done, and where Whagons tells you that something needs you. This guide covers those four, with your real screens, in about seven minutes.

## Try this first

**Three things for your first shift**

1. Open your team's space in the sidebar, then use Filters to show only the tasks assigned to you.
2. Open one of your tasks, read it, and change its status when you actually start the work.
3. Before you stop, leave a comment on the task saying what you did and what's missing.

## 1. Find your work

### A space holds one team's work

**What to do.** In the sidebar, under Spaces, open the space of your team, like Maintenance or Housekeeping. Everything, at the top of the list, shows the tasks of every space you can see.

**Why it matters.** A space is the shared to-do list of a team or an area. You only see the spaces and the tasks you have access to, so Everything means everything you can see, not the whole organization.

**How.** If a space or a task you expect isn't there, tell your supervisor. Usually you aren't on that team yet, or the task belongs to a place that is assigned to someone else.

### Show only your tasks

**What to do.** Open Filters, choose yourself under Assignee and use Apply. Clear filters brings the whole list back.

**Why it matters.** The team's list is long and changes all day. Filtered to you, it is your shift: what's waiting, what's in progress, what's late.

**How.** Start every shift this way. When yours are done, clear the filter and see what's still open for the team.

### Read a task without opening it

**What to do.** Look at a row: its number under ID, the Name, its Status and Priority, who it's assigned to, the dates, and the Spot where it happens.

**Why it matters.** A good task answers four questions at a glance: what, where, who and by when. When one of them is missing, that's usually the question someone will call you about.

**How.** Click a task's name to open it. The columns can change from one space to another, because each team sets up its own.

## 2. Report something in under a minute

### Create a task

**What to do.** Use Create Task at the top of the screen. A panel opens with the title Create New Task.

**Why it matters.** "Can you look at room 214?" said in the hallway gets forgotten. A task has a number, an owner, a status and a history, and it is still there for the next shift.

**How.** Depending on your space, you write a Name or choose a template with From template. A template fills in the name, the priority and who it goes to for you.

### Write it so nobody has to call you

**What to do.** Write the name as action, object and place. Choose the place under Spot. Drop a photo under Attachments. Add a Description only when the name and the photo aren't enough.

**Why it matters.** The person who reads it may be on another shift, on another floor. With the place picked as a Spot instead of written in the text, the task reaches the people who look after that place, and the photo answers half the questions before anyone asks.

**How.** Names that work:

```naming
Replace leaking tap · Room 214
Elevator 2 stops between floors
Restock towels · Floor 3 cart
```

### What happens after you save

**What to do.** Nothing else. The panel closes and the task is in its space.

**Why it matters.** Whagons gives the task its number and lets the assigned people know. If nobody is assigned, the people on the space's team get a notification instead, so the task doesn't sit there unseen.

**How.** If you can't see your new task in the list, check your filters. The task is there, but a filter can hide it.

## 3. Move a task from new to done

### The status tells everyone where the work is

**What to do.** In the grid, click the task's status and choose the next one.

**Why it matters.** Status names belong to your organization, for example Pending, In Progress and Completed. Behind each name, Whagons knows whether it means working, paused or finished, and the menu only offers the steps your team allows.

**How.** Change it when the work really starts, not when you first see the task. Starting a task can add you to its assignees, so everyone knows who is on it.

### Leave the trail on the task

**What to do.** Open the task, go to Comments, write what you did and what's missing, and add a photo with Attach file.

**Why it matters.** The next shift reads the task, not your chat. Each comment stays on the task with your name and the time, and photos of the result close arguments before they start.

**How.** Enter sends the comment and Shift+Enter starts a new line. Writing someone's name in a task comment doesn't notify them yet, so when it's urgent, tell them directly as well.

### Finish only when it's done

**What to do.** Before you choose the final status, check the form, the photos and any signature or approval the task asks for.

**Why it matters.** A finished task is read-only: the panel says "Finished tasks are read-only." After that, nobody can change its status, its assignees or its form answers.

**How.** If you have to stop before it's done, use your team's paused or waiting status and leave a comment saying why. That way the task doesn't look abandoned.

## 4. Hear when something needs you

### Notifications live behind your avatar

**What to do.** Click your avatar at the top right. The number on it counts what you haven't read, and Notifications lists everything, newest first.

**Why it matters.** You get one when a task is assigned to you, when a task with no one assigned arrives in your team's space, when an approval or an acknowledgment needs you, and for alerts your organization set up, like an SLA about to run out.

**How.** Click a notification to go straight to the task. On your phone, allow notifications when Whagons asks, so they reach you with the app closed.

## On your phone

On a narrow screen, a space becomes a simple list. Tap a task to open it, use Search tasks... at the top, and Load More to see more. Everything in this guide works the same way, one tap at a time.

## Habits that make teams fast

- **One problem, one task.** Two problems in one task end up closed half done.
- **Photo first.** One before, one after. Nobody has to walk back to check.
- **Hand over in the task.** "Checked the valve, need part X, room is locked after 4 pm."
- **Start the shift filtered to you, end it on the team.** Clear the filter and pick up what's left.
- **Supervisors: group by Status or by Spot.** You'll see in seconds where work is piling up.
- **Use the space's chat for the team, comments for the task.** The space chat lives under Collab, and the task's history stays with the task.

## Your first-day checklist

**Finding**

- [ ] I can open my team's space
- [ ] I can filter the list to my own tasks

**Reporting**

- [ ] My task names say what, and where
- [ ] I choose the Spot and add a photo

**Working**

- [ ] I change the status when the work starts
- [ ] I leave a comment before I stop
- [ ] I finish a task only when it's done

**Hearing back**

- [ ] I know my notifications are behind my avatar
