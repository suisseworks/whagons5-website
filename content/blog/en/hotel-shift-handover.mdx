A hotel never closes, but its people go home. Every few hours the front desk, housekeeping and maintenance hand the building to someone else, and that handover is where a lot of work disappears. The morning shift knows that Room 214 has complained about the AC twice. If that leaves with them, the afternoon shift finds out from the guest. A good shift handover is short, written, and answers the same questions every time: what is still open, who owns it, by when, and what could upset a guest. This guide gives you a template to start from and shows how to keep it in Whagons instead of in a notebook.

## Try this first

**Three things for this week**

1. Sit in on two handovers in different departments and write down what was said, and what was only implied.
2. The next morning, check which of those items were finished, which were forgotten and which were done twice.
3. Agree on one written format for one department, like the front desk, and use it for every shift change for a week.

## 1. What a good handover answers

### What is still open

**What to do.** List only the items the next shift must act on or watch.

**Why it matters.** A handover that repeats the whole shift is too long to read at 15:00 with guests at the desk. The open items are the ones that get lost.

### Who owns each item

**What to do.** Give every open item one name. If the owner is leaving, the name changes to someone on the incoming shift before they go.

**Why it matters.** "Maintenance knows" is not an owner. When an item belongs to a department instead of a person, everyone assumes someone else has it.

### By when, and where

**What to do.** Add a time and a place to each item: a room number, a floor, a public area.

**Why it matters.** "AC noise" means nothing at the end of a long day. "AC noise, Room 214, before 16:00" can be picked up by anyone.

### What touches a guest

**What to do.** Mark the items a guest is waiting on or has already complained about: repeat complaints, promised callbacks, late checkouts, special requests, VIP arrivals.

**Why it matters.** These are the items where a miss turns into a bad review. They should be read first, not found halfway down a list.

## 2. A template for each department

Keep the same four columns everywhere: item, place, owner and time, plus a guest flag. What changes is what each department tends to carry over.

**Front desk.** Guests with open complaints, promised callbacks, late checkouts, early arrivals, room moves and pending payments.

**Housekeeping.** Rooms not yet released, rooms on hold or out of order, special requests like extra beds or cots, and lost and found items still to log.

**Maintenance.** Open repairs with their status, rooms blocked by a repair, parts on order, contractors expected on site, and checks from the [preventive maintenance schedule](/en/blog/preventive-maintenance-schedule) that did not get done.

```naming
Check AC noise · Room 214 · Luis · 16:00 · Guest
Late checkout approved · Room 108 · Ana · 14:00
Replace bulb · Lobby, left column · Night shift
```

## 3. Run the handover from tasks, not from memory

### Make open items tasks before the shift ends

**What to do.** In Whagons, anything on the handover that needs someone to go somewhere and do something becomes a task, with its Spot, an owner and a due time.

**Why it matters.** The handover list then builds itself: it is the open tasks for your department. Nothing depends on who remembered what.

### Read the guest items out loud

**What to do.** Keep a short face-to-face handover, but only for the flagged items and anything unusual. Everything else is already written in the tasks.

**Why it matters.** Talking is the fastest way to share urgency. Five minutes on what matters beats twenty on everything.

### Sign it off

**What to do.** The incoming lead confirms they have read the open items and accepts them, with their name and the time.

**Why it matters.** The sign-off is the moment ownership moves. Without it, the outgoing shift thinks it handed over and the incoming shift thinks it was told nothing.

## Your shift handover checklist

Use it at every shift change for a week.

**Before the shift ends**

- [ ] Every open item has a place, an owner and a time
- [ ] Items that need someone to go and act are tasks, not notes
- [ ] Guest-sensitive items are flagged

**During the handover**

- [ ] Flagged items are read out first
- [ ] Owners leaving the building have been replaced
- [ ] Questions are answered before the outgoing shift leaves

**After the handover**

- [ ] The incoming lead has signed off with name and time
- [ ] Nothing important lives only in a notebook or a chat

Start with the front desk. When a week goes by without a guest repeating a complaint to the next shift, bring in housekeeping and maintenance.
