Almost every hotel runs on a WhatsApp group. Housekeeping, maintenance, the front desk and the managers are all in it, and it works because everyone already has it on their phone. The trouble starts when the group stops being a place to talk and becomes the place where work is assigned. A request for Room 214 goes in at 07:42, three people read it, someone writes "ok", and at 11:20 the front desk is asking whether it was fixed. The group has no way to say who owns a request, where it is, and whether it is finished. This guide explains where group chats fail for hotel operations and how to move the work into tasks while the chat stays for conversation.

## Try this first

**Three things for this week**

1. Scroll back through yesterday in your operations group and count the messages that were actually requests for work.
2. For each one, try to answer: who took it, when was it done, and how do you know?
3. Pick one type of request, like maintenance in guest rooms, and agree that from Monday it becomes a task instead of a message.

## 1. Where requests get lost in a group chat

### No one owns the message

**What to do.** Look at how requests are answered in your group. Usually it is a thumbs up, an "ok" or nothing at all.

**Why it matters.** A message is sent to everyone, so it belongs to no one. An "ok" can mean "I saw it", "I'm going" or "someone should go". When two people think the other one went, the guest waits.

### The next message buries it

**What to do.** Notice how long a request stays visible before new messages push it off the screen.

**Why it matters.** In a busy group, a request from the morning is gone by lunch. The people on the next shift never see it unless someone remembers to repeat it.

### There is no status and no proof

**What to do.** Ask your team how they know a repair is done. Often the answer is "someone said so" or "I went to look".

**Why it matters.** A chat can't show which requests are open, which are late and which are finished. So managers end up asking in the group, which adds more messages.

## 2. Move the work, keep the conversation

### Decide what becomes a task

**What to do.** Write a short rule with your heads of department: anything that needs someone to go somewhere and do something is a task. Questions, warnings and coordination can stay in the chat.

**Why it matters.** You are not banning the group. The rule only takes out the part the group is bad at.

**How.** Start with requests that already have a clear place, like rooms and public areas:

```naming
Replace leaking tap · Room 214
Restock towels · Floor 3 cart
Check AC noise · Room 108
```

### Create it once, with a place and a photo

**What to do.** In Whagons, use Create Task, write the name as action, object and place, choose the place under Spot and add a photo.

**Why it matters.** Picking the Spot sends the task to the people who look after that place, and the photo answers the first questions before anyone calls.

### Let the status do the talking

**What to do.** The person who goes changes the status to In progress when they start and to the finished status when they are done, with a comment or a photo of the result.

**Why it matters.** The front desk no longer has to ask in the group whether 214 is ready. They can look at the task, and the next shift can read what was done without scrolling through anyone's phone.

## 3. Make it stick

### Answer chat requests with a task

**What to do.** When a request still arrives in the group, the supervisor turns it into a task and replies with its number.

**Why it matters.** People learn faster from a reply than from a rule.

### Put repeated work on a schedule

**What to do.** If the same request appears in the chat every week, like a filter change or a generator test, move it to a plan in [Work Plans](/en/blog/work-plans).

**Why it matters.** Recurring work should not depend on someone remembering to post it. The [preventive maintenance schedule](/en/blog/preventive-maintenance-schedule) and the [room readiness checklist](/en/blog/room-readiness-checklist) show how to set those up.

## Your group chat checklist

Go through it with your heads of department before Monday.

**The rule**

- [ ] Everyone knows which requests must be tasks
- [ ] The chat is still open for questions and coordination
- [ ] One type of request is the first to move

**The tasks**

- [ ] Every request has an action, an object and a place
- [ ] The place is chosen as a Spot, not written in the text
- [ ] Statuses change when the work starts and ends

**The habit**

- [ ] Supervisors reply to chat requests with a task number
- [ ] Repeated requests live in Work Plans

Start with one department. When nobody has to ask "did anyone go?" for a week, move the next one.
