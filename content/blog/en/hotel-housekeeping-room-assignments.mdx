At 7:30 the housekeeping office fills up. The supervisor reads out room numbers, someone gets a floor full of checkouts, someone else gets a floor of stayovers, and by noon one attendant is finished while another is still on her sixth departure. The rooms were split by count, and twelve rooms are never just twelve rooms. A fair assignment gives everyone a similar amount of work, in a way the team can see and trust. This guide gives you a simple daily room board to do that.

## Try this first

**Three things for tomorrow morning**

1. Give each type of clean a weight. For example, a checkout counts as 2 credits and a stayover as 1.
2. Add up tomorrow's credits and divide them by the number of attendants on shift. That is the target for each person.
3. Mark VIPs and early arrivals on the board before anyone leaves the office.

## 1. Count work, not rooms

### Credits make the load visible

**What to do.** Agree on a credit for each kind of clean your hotel does: checkout, stayover, deep clean, a suite, a turndown if you do them. Build each attendant's list until it reaches the day's target, not until it reaches a room count.

**Why it matters.** A checkout with a full linen change takes longer than a stayover where the guest asked only for towels. When every room counts as one, the person with the most departures always has the hardest day.

**How.** Start with simple numbers and adjust them after a couple of weeks, using what your supervisors see on the floor. There is no standard that fits every property.

## 2. Keep sections together

### Floors first, then balance

**What to do.** Give each attendant a section, usually a floor or a wing, and then use the extra rooms from other sections to even out the credits.

**Why it matters.** Walking is work too. An attendant whose rooms are spread over four floors spends part of the day in the lift with her cart. And when the same person cleans the same floor, she knows its rooms and its problems.

**How.** On the board, colour each room by attendant. A quick look tells you if someone's rooms are scattered all over the building.

## 3. Put VIPs and early arrivals first

### Priority is part of the assignment

**What to do.** Before the board goes out, mark the rooms the front desk needs first: VIPs, early arrivals, guests with special requests, rooms coming back from [out of order](/en/blog/hotel-out-of-order-rooms).

**Why it matters.** An attendant can't know that 301 has a guest arriving at 11:00 unless the board tells her. Otherwise the room gets cleaned in walking order and the front desk asks for it on the radio.

**How.** Get arrival times from the front desk before the board goes out. Put priority rooms at the top of each list, and spread them so no single attendant carries all the urgent ones.

## 4. Plan for late changes and sick calls

### The board will change, so decide how

**What to do.** Agree on who can change the board after it is released. When someone calls in sick, split her rooms across the team using the same credits, instead of handing the whole section to whoever is closest.

**Why it matters.** Late checkouts, extensions and absences happen every day. If the changes travel by word of mouth, two people clean the same room and another room is forgotten.

**How.** Keep one person, usually the supervisor on shift, as the only one who moves rooms. Every change goes on the board, not just into a chat. Use [WhatsApp groups](/en/blog/whatsapp-groups-hotel-operations) for quick messages and the board as the record.

## 5. Release the board, then follow it

### One release, a clear time

**What to do.** Set a time to release the board every day, and have the supervisor check it before it goes out: credits are balanced, priority rooms are marked, sections make sense.

**Why it matters.** A board released at a fixed time lets the team start without waiting and tells the front desk when to expect rooms.

**How.** In Whagons, each clean can be a task on the room Spot, assigned to one attendant, with its priority. With the [Cleaning powerup](/en/blog/cleaning), Create Cleaning makes one task per room in a single pass, so the morning list is already on everyone's phone. Then check finished rooms with your [room inspection checklist](/en/blog/room-inspection-checklist).

## Your room assignment checklist

Use it every morning for a week, then keep the parts that work.

**Before the board goes out**

- [ ] Credits agreed for each kind of clean
- [ ] Day's credits divided by the attendants on shift
- [ ] Each attendant has a section, with extra rooms only to balance

- [ ] VIPs, early arrivals and special requests marked and spread across the team
- [ ] Board checked by a supervisor and released at the agreed time

**During the day**

- [ ] One person allowed to change the board
- [ ] Sick calls split using credits, not handed to one person
- [ ] Every change recorded on the board

Start with tomorrow's board. When every attendant can look at the list and say "that's a fair day," the system is working.
