A room is not ready because someone cleaned it. It is ready when it was cleaned, checked against a standard, anything broken was reported, and the front desk knows it can be sold. When those steps live on a paper sheet and a WhatsApp group, the gap between "clean" and "released" is where guests end up waiting in the lobby. This guide gives you a hotel room inspection checklist you can copy, and shows how to run the path from checkout to released room in Whagons, one task at a time.

## Try this first

**Three things for your next departure day**

1. Copy the inspection checklist below and cut it down to what your supervisors really check.
2. Agree on one rule: a room is released only after it passes inspection.
3. Give every defect found during cleaning its own task, with the room and a photo.

## 1. Decide what "ready" means

### Write the standard down

**What to do.** Put the inspection standard in one list that every attendant and supervisor uses, in the same order they walk the room.

**Why it matters.** If each supervisor inspects from memory, two people release the same room with different results. A written list makes the inspection repeatable, and it tells the attendant exactly what will be checked.

**How.** Start from this list and adapt it to your room types:

```checklist
Entry: door closes and locks, key card works, no marks on the door
Bed: linen changed, no stains or hair, pillows and throws to standard
Bathroom: toilet, shower and sink disinfected, no hair, drains run
Towels and amenities: full set, to standard, nothing opened
High-touch surfaces: remote, light switches, handles, phone, safe keypad
Floor: vacuumed or mopped, under the bed and behind the door
Air: no odor, AC set to the house temperature, filter grille clean
Lights and devices: every bulb works, TV and remote work, chargers in place
Windows and curtains: clean, curtains open and close
Nothing left behind: drawers, safe and closet empty
Defects: anything broken reported as a task, not worked around
```

### Agree on who releases the room

**What to do.** Name the role that inspects and the moment the room counts as ready.

**Why it matters.** "Clean" and "ready to sell" are different states. Without a clear owner for the last step, the attendant assumes the supervisor will check, and the supervisor assumes the attendant already did.

**How.** In the [Cleaning powerup](/en/blog/cleaning) you define your own cleaning statuses. If supervisors inspect, create an Inspected status and treat only that one as ready. If nobody inspects, don't create it: a status nobody uses only adds noise.

## 2. From checkout to clean

### Turn every departure into a cleaning task

**What to do.** At the start of the day, create one cleaning task for each checkout room.

**Why it matters.** When the cleaning is a task, the room follows it. In Cleaning, each task status maps to a room status, so a room goes from Dirty to Cleaning to Clean as the attendant works, without anyone updating a sheet.

**How.** Use Create Cleaning, pick all the departure rooms under Spots, the checkout cleaning task, the priority and the person. Whagons creates one task per room. The [Cleaning guide](/en/blog/cleaning) covers the setup and the status mapping.

### Report defects instead of working around them

**What to do.** When an attendant finds a dripping tap or a dead bulb, they create a task for maintenance with the room as its Spot and a photo.

**Why it matters.** A defect noticed and not reported comes back as a guest complaint. With the room picked as the Spot, the task reaches the people who look after it, and the photo answers half the questions before anyone asks.

**How.** Name it as action, object and place, for example:

```text
Replace dead bulb · Room 312 · bedside lamp
```

## 3. Inspect before you release

### Inspect against the same list

**What to do.** The supervisor walks the room with the same checklist the attendant used.

**Why it matters.** Inspection is where the standard becomes real. When the list lives on the inspection task as a form, each answer stays on the record with the person and the time, instead of a tick on paper that nobody keeps.

**How.** Attach the checklist as a form to your inspection task template, and ask for a photo of anything that fails.

### Turn a failed item into a corrective action

**What to do.** When an item fails, create follow-up work with an owner and a due time, then re-check it.

**Why it matters.** "Bathroom not to standard" written in a notebook doesn't fix itself. A corrective task with a deadline gets done, and the room stays out of the ready count until it is.

**How.** Keep the room in its not-ready status until the follow-up task is finished and the item passes.

### Make the ready rooms visible

**What to do.** Keep Cleaning Management open where housekeeping and the front desk can see it.

**Why it matters.** Daily progress shows the share of rooms in a ready status right now, and each status card shows its count. The front desk stops calling to ask which room is ready.

**How.** Sort by last cleaned to see the rooms that have gone longest without cleaning first.

## 4. Keep the deeper checks on a schedule

**What to do.** Move the checks that don't happen every day, like mattress rotation, curtain washing or a full room audit, into a routine.

**Why it matters.** Those are the checks that get skipped first on a busy week. A routine creates the task on its own when it is due.

**How.** In [Work Plans](/en/blog/work-plans), build one schedule and select the rooms as spots. Whagons creates one task per room on every run, and the plan's calendar shows which days were late.

## Your room readiness checklist

Go through it with your housekeeping supervisor before the next busy departure day.

**The standard**

- [ ] One written inspection list, in walking order
- [ ] A clear role that inspects and releases rooms

**The flow**

- [ ] Every checkout room gets its own cleaning task
- [ ] Cleaning statuses map to room statuses
- [ ] Defects become maintenance tasks with a Spot and a photo

**The release**

- [ ] Inspection uses the same list, attached as a form
- [ ] Failed items become follow-up tasks with a due time
- [ ] Ready rooms are visible to the front desk

**The routine**

- [ ] Deeper checks run on a schedule in Work Plans

When the front desk can see which room is ready without picking up the radio, the checklist is doing its job.
