On Monday a pipe leaks under the sink in Room 315. Maintenance blocks the room in the system and orders a part. On Thursday the part arrives, the technician fixes the leak and goes on to the next job. Nobody tells housekeeping, nobody tells the front desk, and the room stays blocked through a full weekend. Or the opposite happens: someone releases the room in the morning, and the first guest of the day walks into a bathroom that still has tools on the floor. Out-of-order rooms are rarely a technical problem. They are a coordination problem between three teams. This guide gives you a simple process from the moment a room is blocked to the moment it goes back to sale.

## Try this first

**Three things for this week**

1. Print or open the list of every room blocked right now. Next to each one, write why, who owns it and when it should be back.
2. Agree on who can block a room and who can release it. Write both names or roles down.
3. Agree that no room goes back to sale without a housekeeping clean and an inspection after the repair.

## 1. Use two kinds of block, and use them on purpose

### Out of order and out of service are not the same

**What to do.** Use **out of order** when the room can't be sold at all: a leak, no air conditioning, a broken lock, a renovation. Use **out of service** for a small issue that shouldn't stop a sale for long, like a stained chair or a flickering lamp, when your property chooses to hold the room until it is fixed.

**Why it matters.** Out-of-order rooms usually come out of your available inventory, so they change occupancy and revenue reports. If every small issue becomes out of order, your numbers stop telling the truth. If serious problems are logged as out of service, the room can be sold by mistake.

**How.** Each property management system names these statuses a little differently. Check what yours does with each one and write a one-line rule your team can follow.

## 2. Every block needs a record

### Five fields, every time

**What to do.** When a room is blocked, log the room, the reason, who blocked it, the expected return date and who owns the repair.

**Why it matters.** "Blocked, ask maintenance" is not a record. When the reason and the date are written, the front desk can plan arrivals and the manager can see which rooms have been blocked for too long.

**How.** Link the block to the maintenance task. Use the same levels as your [maintenance request priorities](/en/blog/hotel-maintenance-request-priorities): a room blocked during a busy week usually deserves a higher priority than the same repair in a quiet week.

## 3. One list that all three teams can see

### Maintenance, housekeeping and the front desk

**What to do.** Keep a single list of blocked rooms with a status for each one: blocked, in repair, waiting for a part, ready for cleaning, ready for inspection.

**Why it matters.** The front desk needs to know which rooms are coming back for tonight's arrivals. Housekeeping needs to know which rooms will need a deep clean after the work. Maintenance needs to know which room to finish first.

**How.** Review the list every morning in a two-minute check, and mention every blocked room at the [shift handover](/en/blog/hotel-shift-handover). Any room past its expected date gets a new date and a reason.

## 4. Return the room to sale only when it is really ready

### Fixed is not the same as ready

**What to do.** When maintenance finishes, the room goes to housekeeping, not straight to sale. After the clean, a supervisor inspects it, and only then does the person allowed to release it put it back on sale.

**Why it matters.** Repairs leave dust, tools, moved furniture and sometimes a smell. A room released too early becomes the next guest complaint.

**How.** Use your [room inspection checklist](/en/blog/room-inspection-checklist) with one extra line: test the thing that was repaired. Run the tap, turn on the air conditioning, try the lock.

## 5. Look at the pattern once a month

### The same room, the same fault

**What to do.** Once a month, look at which rooms were blocked, for how long and why.

**Why it matters.** A room blocked three times for the same leak needs a different fix, not a fourth repair. Recurring faults are a good start for your [preventive maintenance schedule](/en/blog/preventive-maintenance-schedule).

## Your out-of-order room checklist

Use it for every blocked room this week.

**When a room is blocked**

- [ ] Correct status chosen: out of order or out of service
- [ ] Reason, who blocked it, expected return date and repair owner logged
- [ ] Repair task created with a priority

**While it is blocked**

- [ ] Room visible on the shared list with its current status
- [ ] Blocked rooms reviewed in the morning check and at shift handover
- [ ] New date and reason given to any room past its expected return

**Before it goes back to sale**

- [ ] Repair finished and tested
- [ ] Room cleaned by housekeeping and inspected by a supervisor
- [ ] Room released by the person allowed to release it

Start with today's list of blocked rooms. When anyone on shift can answer "when is 315 coming back, and why is it blocked?" in a minute, the process is working.
