A guest comes down at seven in the evening: the air conditioning in Room 207 isn't cooling. The front desk apologises, calls maintenance on the radio and promises someone will go up. An hour later the guest calls again, nobody knows whether the technician went, and the night shift hears about it for the first time at handover. The problem was small. The way it was handled made it big. A guest complaint process doesn't remove problems, but it makes sure every complaint has a record, an owner and an ending. This guide covers it from the first word to the follow-up.

## Try this first

**Three things for this week**

1. Agree that every complaint gets logged, even the ones fixed on the spot. One line is enough.
2. Decide who owns the guest in each shift: the person who took the complaint or the shift supervisor. Write it down.
3. Promise only times you control. "I'm sending maintenance now and I'll call you in twenty minutes" beats "it will be fixed soon".

## 1. Listen first, then log it

### Let the guest finish

**What to do.** Listen without interrupting, repeat the problem back in your own words and apologise for the inconvenience without arguing about whose fault it is.

### One record per complaint, written right away

**What to do.** Log the room, the guest's name, what happened, when and who took it.

**Why it matters.** A complaint that only lives in someone's memory disappears at the end of the shift. A record means the next person can pick it up without asking the guest to start over.

## 2. Give every complaint one owner

### The fix and the guest are two jobs

**What to do.** Send the fix to the team that does it (maintenance, housekeeping, food and beverage) and keep the guest with one person at the front desk or the duty manager.

**Why it matters.** The technician can fix the AC, but they won't call the guest back. When the guest has one named contact, nobody assumes someone else did it.

**How.** For maintenance problems, use the same levels as your [maintenance request priorities](/en/blog/hotel-maintenance-request-priorities). A guest in the room right now usually means Urgent.

## 3. Keep the guest updated

### A promise with a time on it

**What to do.** Tell the guest what will happen next and when you will contact them again. Then do it, even if the news is "the technician is still working on it".

**How.** If the fix will take long, offer a choice the guest can accept or decline: a fan for now, another room, or a later time that suits them. What you offer as compensation is your property's decision; write down who can approve what.

## 4. Close the loop

### Fixed is not the same as closed

**What to do.** When the team reports the problem fixed, the guest owner checks with the guest that it is fixed for them. Only then does the complaint get closed, with a short note of what was done.

**Why it matters.** A complaint marked fixed while the guest still feels ignored is a bad review waiting to happen.

### Pass open complaints to the next shift

**What to do.** Every open complaint goes into the [shift handover](/en/blog/hotel-shift-handover) with its status and the next promised contact time.

## 5. Learn from the pattern

### Review the log once a week

**What to do.** Once a week, a manager reads the closed complaints and looks for repeats: the same room, the same hour, the same department.

**Why it matters.** Three noise complaints about the same corridor are not three bad luck moments. They are one problem that needs a fix, a rule or a conversation.

**How.** In Whagons, a complaint can be a task on the room or area Spot, with the guest's words, who owns it, the team doing the fix and a status everyone can see: New, In progress, Fixed, Closed. It doesn't get lost in a [WhatsApp group](/en/blog/whatsapp-groups-hotel-operations), and the history of a room shows its repeat problems at a glance:

```text
AC not cooling · Room 207 · In progress · Carla 19:05
Noise in the corridor · Room 412 · New · Front desk
```

## Your guest complaint checklist

Use it for every complaint this week.

**When the guest complains**

- [ ] Guest listened to without interruption and the problem repeated back
- [ ] Complaint logged with room, guest, what happened, time and who took it
- [ ] One guest owner named for this complaint

**While it is being fixed**

- [ ] Fix sent to the right team with a priority
- [ ] Guest told what happens next and when they will hear back
- [ ] Promised contact made on time

**When it is fixed**

- [ ] Guest confirmed the problem is solved for them
- [ ] Complaint closed with a short note of what was done
- [ ] Open complaints passed on at shift handover

Start by logging every complaint for one week. When anyone on shift can answer "what happened with the guest in 207?" in a minute, the process is working.
