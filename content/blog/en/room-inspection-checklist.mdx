Housekeeping cleaned the room. That is not the same as ready for the guest. A stain on the duvet, hair in the drain, a dead bulb or a stale smell will be found by the guest first. A hotel room inspection checklist is the short, fixed walk a supervisor or QA person takes after cleaning, so issues are caught, fixed and re-checked before the front desk sells the room. This guide covers that walk — not the full [checkout-to-released flow](/en/blog/room-readiness-checklist), but the inspection step that makes that flow honest.

## Try this first

**Three things for this week**

1. Walk three rooms with a supervisor and write down every item they actually check, in the order they walk.
2. Cut that list to twelve items or fewer. Anything that takes longer than a quick look goes on a deeper audit, not on every inspection.
3. Agree one rule: a fail means the room is not released until the item is fixed and re-checked.

## 1. What a good inspection covers

Keep the list in walking order so the supervisor never jumps from bathroom to window and back.

### Bathroom

**What to do.** Check toilet, shower, sink, mirrors, drains, towels and amenities for hair, marks, leftover packaging and missing pieces.

**Why it matters.** Most "dirty room" complaints start in the bathroom. If the drain still holds hair from the last stay, cleaning was not finished.

### Beds and soft surfaces

**What to do.** Look at linen, pillows, duvet and under-bed edges for stains, hair and wrinkles that break the house standard.

**Why it matters.** Guests notice the bed first. A crease they can live with; a hair they will not.

### Amenities, lights, AC, smell and safety

**What to do.** Confirm the amenity set matches the room type; wipe-check remotes, switches and handles; turn every light on; set the AC to the house temperature; smell the room from the door; check that the door closes and locks.

**Why it matters.** Missing shampoo, a sticky remote, a dead bulb or a musty smell are free complaints. A door that does not latch is a safety issue, not a cosmetic one.

## 2. Who inspects, and when

### Name the role

**What to do.** Decide who inspects: a floor supervisor, a dedicated QA person, or the head of housekeeping on sample days. Write the role next to the checklist.

**Why it matters.** If "someone" inspects, nobody owns the fail. The attendant assumes the supervisor will catch it; the supervisor assumes the attendant already did.

### Inspect after cleaning, before release

**What to do.** Start only when cleaning is done. Do not release the room until inspection passes.

**Why it matters.** Inspecting while the attendant is still in the room wastes both people. Releasing before inspection turns every miss into a guest problem.

**How.** Tie inspection to the room statuses you use in [Cleaning](/en/blog/cleaning): Clean is not Inspected, and only Inspected (or your ready status) is sellable.

## 3. Pass, fail and the rework loop

### Mark pass or fail, then send fails back as work

**What to do.** Each item gets a clear pass or fail. When an item fails, create follow-up for housekeeping or maintenance with the room as the Spot, a short description and a photo. Keep the room out of the ready count until that work is done and the item is re-checked by the same role.

**Why it matters.** "Bathroom not to standard" in a notebook does not fix itself. A task with an owner, a due time and a photo does — and without a re-check, rework is a hope, not a control.

**How.** In Whagons, turn each fail into a task on the room Spot:

```text
Clear hair from shower drain · Room 412
Replace dead bulb · Room 412 · bedside lamp
```

## 4. Keep the checklist short and fixed

### One list, same order

**What to do.** Use one core list for stayover and checkout cleans. Add a short appendix for room types that need extras (cribs, connecting doors, kitchenettes), not a second full checklist.

**Why it matters.** Long lists get skipped on busy days. Short fixed lists get used, and they make training new supervisors faster.

### Sampling, recording and handover

**What to do.** Decide when you inspect every room and when you sample (full on VIP, long-stay and problem rooms; sample on quiet midweek days if volume forces it). Keep who inspected, when, which items failed and the photos on the inspection task, and pass open rework into [shift handover](/en/blog/hotel-shift-handover).

**Why it matters.** A fake 100% rule creates false confidence. A checklist that lives only in someone's head cannot be audited — and open rework that never hits the next shift vanishes.

## Your room inspection checklist

Use it after every clean you decide to inspect this week.

**Before you walk**

- [ ] Cleaning is marked done for this room
- [ ] You have the short fixed list in walking order
- [ ] You know who owns rework if something fails

**During the walk**

- [ ] Bathroom: toilet, shower, sink, drains, towels, amenities
- [ ] Bed and soft surfaces: linen, stains, hair
- [ ] Amenities and high-touch: set complete, remotes and switches
- [ ] Lights, AC, smell, door lock and safety

**After the walk**

- [ ] Every fail is a task with Spot and photo
- [ ] Room stays not-ready until rework is done and re-checked
- [ ] Pass is recorded with inspector name and time

Start with one floor for a week. When fails drop, expand the same list to the rest of the house.
