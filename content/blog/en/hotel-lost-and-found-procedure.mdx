A guest calls from the airport: "I think I left my sunglasses in the room." The front desk asks housekeeping, housekeeping thinks someone found something on the second floor, and the item turns up in a back-office drawer, or not at all. A lost and found process doesn't need to be complicated. It needs every found item to have a record, a place and an owner. This guide goes from the moment an item is found to the moment it goes back to the guest.

## Try this first

**Three things for this week**

1. Pick one place where found items are stored, with a lock and numbered shelves or bins. Close every other "temporary" drawer.
2. Agree on the five details every found item needs: what it is, where it was found, when, who found it, and where it is stored now.
3. Name one person per shift who answers lost and found calls and has the key.

## 1. Log it the moment it is found

### One record per item, written on the spot

**What to do.** When housekeeping, maintenance or anyone else finds an item, they log it right away: a short description, the room or area, the time and their name. A photo helps more than a long description.

**Why it matters.** Items often get lost a second time between the room and the storage place. A record made on the spot means the item exists before it leaves the room.

**How.** Keep it plain and specific: "Black sunglasses, gold frame" beats "glasses". Don't guess the brand or value.

### Items found in occupied rooms

**What to do.** If the guest is still staying, leave the item where it is. Only items from checked-out rooms or public areas go to lost and found.

## 2. Label, bag and store it in one place

### A tag that matches the record

**What to do.** Put each item in its own bag with a tag showing the record number, the room or area and the date. Store it in the locked lost and found area on the shelf or bin the record points to.

**Why it matters.** When the guest calls, anyone on shift goes straight to the right shelf.

**How.** Keep valuables, documents and cash separate, in a safe, with a second person present when they are logged. Your property's own policy decides who can open the safe.

## 3. Answer the guest quickly and honestly

### Search the log, not the building

**What to do.** When a guest calls or writes, search the log by room, date and description. If there is a match, confirm details only the owner would know, such as the colour, a scratch or what is inside a bag, before you promise anything.

**Why it matters.** A fast answer makes the guest feel looked after. Checking ownership stops you handing the item to the wrong person.

**How.** If nothing matches, say so, record the request with the guest's contact details, and check new entries against open requests.

## 4. Return it and close the record

### Every return leaves a trace

**What to do.** Record how the item went back: picked up at the desk, sent by courier at the guest's cost, or held for their next stay. Note who handed it over, when, and the guest's confirmation or signature.

**Why it matters.** An item without a closing record looks lost, even when it was returned.

### Decide what happens to unclaimed items

**What to do.** Set a holding period for each type of item and what happens after it: donation, disposal or handing it to the authorities. Check local rules before you write it down, and treat documents and valuables separately.

**Why it matters.** Without a rule, the storage room fills up and nobody wants to be the one who throws things away.

## 5. Make it part of the daily routine

### Review open items at handover

**What to do.** At each [shift handover](/en/blog/hotel-shift-handover), mention new items and guest requests still open. Once a week, a supervisor checks that every stored item has a record and every record has an item.

**Why it matters.** A short check catches the bag on the wrong shelf before the guest calls.

**How.** In Whagons, a found item can be a task on the room or area Spot, with a photo, the person who found it and a status the whole team can see: Logged, Stored, Asked for, Returned. It doesn't get buried in a [WhatsApp group](/en/blog/whatsapp-groups-hotel-operations), and the [room inspection](/en/blog/room-inspection-checklist) can include a quick "check drawers and under the bed" item so fewer things are missed:

```text
Sunglasses + charger · Room 214 · Logged · Ana 11:40
Black umbrella · Lobby · Stored · Shelf B
```

## Your lost and found checklist

Use it for every item found this week.

**When an item is found**

- [ ] Logged on the spot with description, place, time and who found it
- [ ] Photo taken when it helps
- [ ] Left in place if the guest is still staying

**When it is stored**

- [ ] Bagged with a tag that matches the record
- [ ] Stored on the shelf or bin the record points to
- [ ] Valuables and documents in the safe, logged with a second person

**When a guest asks**

- [ ] Log searched by room, date and description
- [ ] Ownership confirmed before anything is promised
- [ ] Return method, date and who handed it over recorded

Start with one locked place and one log. When the front desk can answer "do you have my sunglasses?" in a minute, the process is working.
